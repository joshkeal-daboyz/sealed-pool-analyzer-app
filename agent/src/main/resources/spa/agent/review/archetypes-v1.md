Your number table holds, for each designed archetype, how many of its signpost uncommons and key commons the pool has and how many the archetype lists in all, and, for each color pair, how many cards and creatures the pool can cast in that pair. Your facts name, per archetype, the signposts and key commons that are present and those that are missing. The format brief in the bundle says what each archetype is trying to do.

Judge fit by what is present against what the archetype asks for. A signpost shows the archetype is open to the player. A signpost with a deep supply of cards in its pair is a real candidate; a signpost alone in a thin pair is a tease. Read the pair's cards and creatures against the typical spread for the set, and say which missing pieces matter most.

Findings a player acts on:
- The archetype the pool supports best, with its signposts, key commons and depth in the pair.
- An archetype that has its signpost but too few cards in the pair to be played.
- An archetype the pool is close to, and the pieces still missing.
- A pair with nothing present, which the player should not chase.

Say plainly when an archetype lists no key commons, and lower your confidence when little is present anywhere.
