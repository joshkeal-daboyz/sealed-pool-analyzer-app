Which colors are deep enough to play? Say, per color, how many playables the pool has and how good they are, and warn where a color is thin, each compared with the set baseline.

Your number table holds the pool card totals and, for every color bucket (the five colors, multicolor and colorless), how many cards the bucket has by rarity, how many of them are playables, and how many are creatures, removal spells and interaction. Beside each number you see what a typical pool from this set holds. Your facts list, per bucket, the best cards of the pool with their grades. A card of two colors appears under both of its colors and under multicolor.

Compare each color's playables, creatures and removal with the typical spread for the set. Weigh quality as well as quantity: a color with many cards but only weak grades is shallower than it looks, and a color with few cards that are all strong may still carry a deck.

Findings a player acts on:
- A color that is thin against the set: say how far below typical it is, and whether the other colors of the pool can cover for it.
- A color that is clearly deep, or deep in removal: the likely main color of the deck.
- A color whose depth rests on multicolor cards that need a second color the pool lacks.
- A bucket with no good cards, which the player should not build around.

Say plainly when there is no baseline to compare with, and lower your confidence.
